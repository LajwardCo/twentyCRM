How it works: the watermark shifts each pixel it covers by about 3 of 255 steps, which is far below what an eye picks up but is a real, exact difference in the file. Subtracting a blurred copy of the image removes the interface itself and leaves those small local differences, and the gain multiplies what is left. Radius should stay a little larger than the stroke width of the watermark text. Photo of a screen averages neighbouring pixels first, which trades sharpness for a chance against camera noise and moiré — a photo often will not give up the mark at all. In an ordinary image editor the equivalent move is a Levels adjustment with the input range pulled to roughly 246–255 over a flat white area of the screenshot.
